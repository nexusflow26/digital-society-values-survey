Values under water: 2026 vs 2060

How does society balance money, nature and each other today, and how might that change in a flooded future? Two dots, about two minutes.

Before you start

You'll place one dot on each of two triangles: one for how much importance society places on three values today, and one for how it will prioritise them in 2060, in the future world described below.

  • Economic and financial security: stable jobs, income, savings, property and wealth.
  • Environment: protecting the climate, land, water and nature.
  • Community: solidarity, looking after neighbours and shared public life.

This survey is anonymous. We don't collect your name, email or any personal details, only where you place the two dots. Responses are used for a university group project. You can submit once per browser.

How to read the triangle

Each corner is one value. The closer your dot is to a corner, the more important that value is. A dot right on a corner means 100% that value; a dot in the very centre means all three matter equally (33% each). The three percentages always add up to 100%.

Click or tap to place your dot, then drag to fine-tune. On a keyboard, focus the triangle and use the arrow keys.

Step 2 of 2 · The year 2060

Now imagine the world of 2060

By 2060, as extreme climate change became apparent, with rising sea levels and flooding, private insurers began refusing to insure the worst-affected land. This had a knock-on effect: no insurance meant no mortgages, causing a crash in the property market. Houses in these areas became practically worthless, driving up prices further inland and causing an influx of migrants moving inland. Coastal cities became full of people too poor to leave, minimally governed and severely neglected. The contrast not only in life expectancy but also in available facilities and quality of life grew strikingly obvious, driven by the varying population density.

In this world of 2060, how much importance will society place on each value?
